1 Logic Operations

There are 4 operators, they are ¬\neg¬, ∧\land∧, ∨\lor∨, ⇒\Rightarrow⇒, the outcome of the operation is the Truth Table.

It might be confused for A⇒BA\Rightarrow BA⇒B, when A=0A=0A=0, no matter what the value of B is, the outcome of the operation is 1. This is because when precondition A is false, B will never violate A.

A⇒BA\Rightarrow BA⇒B: If it rains today (A), I will bring an umbrella (B)

When A=0A=0A=0, which means it doesn’t rain today, then if I take the umbrella (B=1), I don’t violate the promise, because we cannot verify whether I will take it when it rains. Also, if I didn’t take the umbrella (B=0), I still don’t violate.

When A⇒BA\Rightarrow BA⇒B, we say, B is a necessary condition of A (because when B=0 then A=0, we cannot go without B), A is an adequate condition of B (because A alone will decide B), and A⇔BA\Leftrightarrow BA⇔B means A is both necessary and adequate for B and vice versa.

2 Set and Its Operators

Russell’s Paradox: Consider a set R={X∣X∉X}R=\{X|X\notin X\}R={X∣X∈/X}, which means it contains sets that doesn’t contain themselves, then

  • If R∈RR\in RR∈R, then R should satisfy X∉XX\notin XX∈/X, hence R∉RR\notin RR∈/R
  • If R∉RR\notin RR∈/R, then R satisfies X∉XX\notin XX∈/X, hence R∈RR\in RR∈R

This creates a logical contradiction!
